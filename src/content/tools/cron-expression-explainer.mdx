faq:
  - q: What is the difference between Unix, Quartz and AWS EventBridge cron?
    a: Unix cron has 5 fields. Quartz has 6 or 7 - a seconds field in front and an optional year at the end - and numbers weekdays 1 (Sunday) to 7 (Saturday). EventBridge has 6 fields - no seconds, but a required year - and also numbers weekdays 1 to 7. Both Quartz and EventBridge require a `?` in either day-of-month or day-of-week. Pasting the wrong dialect is the commonest source of off-by-one-field schedules, which is why this tool makes you choose and rejects a mismatch with a clear error.
  - q: What do ?, L, W and # mean in Quartz and EventBridge?
    a: 'A `?` means "no specific value" and goes in whichever of day-of-month or day-of-week you are not using. `L` is the last day of the month (or, after a weekday number such as `6L`, the last such weekday - the last Friday). `L-3` is three days before the last day. `15W` is the weekday nearest the 15th without leaving the month, and `LW` is the last weekday. `2#3` is the third Monday. All of these are explained in the description and honoured when the next runs are calculated.'
  - q: What happens when both day-of-month and day-of-week are set in Unix cron?
    a: 'This is the single most common cron misunderstanding: when both fields are restricted (not "*"), standard Unix cron treats them as OR, not AND, so the job runs when either condition is true. "0 0 1 * 1" runs on the 1st of the month and on every Monday, not only on Mondays that happen to be the 1st. Quartz and EventBridge avoid the trap by refusing to accept both, which is what the required `?` is for.'
  - q: What time zone are the next-run times in?
    a: Whichever you choose in the selector - your browser's zone by default, or UTC and a list of major cities. The calculation follows that zone's clock, including daylight saving. A time that daylight saving skips, such as 02:30 on a spring-forward night, does not run that day, and a time that is repeated on a fall-back night runs once. AWS EventBridge evaluates cron in UTC unless the rule sets another zone, so check the zone your own scheduler uses.
  - q: Can I wrap a range around midnight, like 22-2?
    a: Quartz and EventBridge accept it, so `22-2` in the hour field means 22, 23, 0, 1 and 2. Standard Unix cron implementations are inconsistent about it, so this tool rejects a backwards range in Unix mode and suggests listing the hours explicitly, for example `22,23,0,1,2`, which every scheduler understands.
  - q: Does it understand AWS rate() expressions?
    a: Yes, in EventBridge mode. `rate(5 minutes)` is explained as "Every 5 minutes", and the tool checks the singular/plural rule (`rate(1 minute)`, not `rate(1 minutes)`). A rate has no calendar - it counts from when the rule is created - so no list of run times is shown.

## The classic cron mistake

`0 30 8 * * *` - six fields - is a schedule for a different system (Quartz or Spring, which
count seconds first) accidentally typed into a five-field crontab. A lenient parser would
silently read the first `0` as minutes and shift every other field by one position, producing
a schedule that runs at the wrong time with no error at all. Choose the right syntax above and
this tool tells you exactly which dialect an expression belongs to instead of guessing.

## Writing the same schedule in each dialect

Every weekday at 09:15 looks different in each system:

| Syntax | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Unix crontab | `15 9 * * 1-5` |
| Quartz / Spring | `0 15 9 ? * MON-FRI` |
| AWS EventBridge | `15 9 ? * MON-FRI *` |

Note the extra leading `0` for seconds in Quartz, the `?` standing in for day-of-month, and
the trailing `*` for the year in EventBridge. Convert a schedule between systems by checking
the plain-English description matches in each mode.
