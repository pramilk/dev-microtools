faq:
  - q: Are my images uploaded to a server?
    a: No. The images are read and the PDF is written entirely in your browser, in a background worker so the page stays responsive. Nothing you choose is transmitted anywhere — you can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool still works.
  - q: Does converting to PDF reduce the image quality?
    a: Not for JPEG and PNG. A PDF can store JPEG data natively, so JPEG photos are placed in the PDF byte for byte, with no re-compression at all. PNG images are stored losslessly. Other formats (WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP) can't go into a PDF directly, so they're decoded and stored as a 92% quality JPEG if they're opaque — visually identical, and far smaller than raw pixels — or losslessly if they have transparency.
  - q: Why was my phone photo sideways in other converters but upright here?
    a: Phone cameras usually save the pixels in the sensor's orientation and add an EXIF tag saying how to rotate them for display. Image viewers follow that tag, but PDF viewers never do, so a converter that copies the JPEG in naively produces a sideways page. This tool reads the tag and rotates the page drawing to match, without touching the photo's data. If an image is still the wrong way round, use the ↻ button on its row.
  - q: Which page size should I choose?
    a: A4 or Letter if the PDF will be printed or submitted somewhere that expects standard pages — each image is scaled to fit inside the margins and centred, and Auto orientation gives wide images a landscape page. Choose Fit to image for screenshots and designs, where you want each page to be exactly its image with no white border or scaling; the page is sized at 96 pixels per inch, so it opens at the size the image looked on screen.
  - q: Can I convert HEIC photos from an iPhone?
    a: Only in Safari, which is the one browser that can decode HEIC. Elsewhere a HEIC image is flagged as unreadable and left out, while the rest of the batch still converts. On an iPhone you can choose Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible to take JPEG photos instead, or share the photo as JPEG. GIFs contribute their first frame, and SVG files need exporting as PNG first.
  - q: How many images can I add, and how big can the PDF get?
    a: Up to 200 images and 300 MB in total per PDF. Because JPEGs are embedded unchanged, the PDF ends up about the size of the photos you added. If it's too large to email, run it through the PDF Compressor afterwards, or shrink the photos first with the Image Compressor.

## How the images are placed on the page

With **A4** or **Letter**, every image is scaled — up or down — to fill the space inside the
margins while keeping its proportions, then centred. **Auto** orientation picks a landscape
page for a wide image and a portrait page for a tall one, so nothing ends up tiny in the
middle of the wrong-shaped page. Forcing **Portrait** or **Landscape** gives every page the
same orientation, which is usually what you want for printing a set of photos.

With **Fit to image**, there is no fixed paper size: each page is cut to its own image, which
suits screenshots, slides and diagrams. The margin setting still applies, as a white border
around the image.

## What powers this tool

The PDF is written with [`pdf-lib`](https://github.com/Hopding/pdf-lib) (MIT licensed), with
this site's own lossless image encoder (using [`fflate`](https://github.com/101arrowz/fflate),
MIT licensed) for PNG-style images. Decoding WebP, AVIF, GIF and BMP uses your browser's
built-in image codecs. All of it runs locally on your device.
